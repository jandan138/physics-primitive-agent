\begin{abstract}
Automatic primitive collider generation can produce geometry-plausible packages that still fail once they enter a physics engine: compound center-of-mass and inertia accounting, contact behavior, and robot articulation constraints are not visible from mesh fit alone.
We propose a \simchecked{} primitive collider compiler for Newton workflows that treats candidate packages as safety-affecting artifacts until named diagnostics accept, reject, or route them to fallback.
CPD-style primitive decomposition and convex decomposition methods remain candidate generators and baselines; our contribution is the downstream acceptance layer that closes the loop with executable simulation checks.
Recorded evidence on capped bed and Franka assets shows a geometry-passing bed cylinder can fail drop/settle only in a full compound package because of body-state sensitivity, while smaller Franka cylinder packages pass under their recorded gates.
A scoped collision-only microbenchmark on the bed asset reports $2.21\times$ higher generated-contact throughput for accepted Newton-native boxes than for same-count convex-mesh proxies in one pressure scene.
We do not claim broad benchmark superiority, whole-robot manipulation validity, deployment readiness, or safety certification; Phase~0 work targets a reproducible multi-asset diagnostic proof point.
\end{abstract}
