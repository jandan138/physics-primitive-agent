\section{Claim Boundary and Limitations}

The supplement closes by restating the boundary in table form. The main paper's evidence supports
a scoped diagnostic-checker story: primitive packages should be treated as candidates until named
Newton probes accept them under recorded settings. The evidence does not support stronger claims
about deployment, certification, real-world transfer, or broad robot operation.

\paragraph{Unsupported claim classes.}
The supplement should not be read as claiming a production compiler, calibrated default selector,
complete replacement of convex decomposition, or broad primitive speed superiority. It also does
not claim whole-robot Franka joint-performance validity, manipulation validity, real-world
transfer, deployment readiness, safety certification, or a safety proof. The phrase diagnostic
checker is used deliberately instead of formal-verification wording.

\paragraph{Evidence needed for stronger benchmark claims.}
A broader benchmark claim would require a fixed asset split, source and license metadata, paired
asset-level metrics, baseline versions and parameters, Newton version and solver settings,
hardware, seeds, fallback examples, failure examples, and statistical treatment for comparative
claims. The current supplement does not add such a benchmark.

\paragraph{Evidence needed for stronger robot claims.}
A stronger robot claim would require more robot assets or tasks, proof that generated packages are
consumed by robot task runtimes, link/joint graph preservation, source-shape suppression, generated
self-collision accounting, gravity holds, scripted trajectories, self-collision sanity,
end-effector sanity, and contact-operation smokes when contact operation is claimed. The current
Franka material is a scoped generated-package consumption smoke.

\paragraph{Evidence needed for safety or deployment claims.}
Simulator diagnostics can support review of safety-affecting artifacts, but they do not create a
deployment protocol or certification standard. A safety or deployment claim would require a
separate verification standard, environment assumptions, runtime monitoring plan, human review
procedure, and external evidence record.

\paragraph{Why this checklist appears in the supplement.}
The checklist is included to make review easier, not to create future-work claims. A reviewer can
compare any tempting interpretation against the table: if a statement would require one of these
missing evidence classes, it is outside the current paper. This convention is especially important
for primitive collider packages because they are safety-affecting artifacts that may look simple
while changing simulated body state, contact behavior, or robot link ownership.

\paragraph{Final reading rule.}
The intended conclusion is conservative: candidate primitive packages are useful because they are
editable and can be fast after acceptance, but they should be promoted only after named diagnostic
checks accept them. The supplement teaches how those checks are assembled; it does not broaden what
the checks prove.
