\section{Compound Body-State Mechanism}

Geometry-only selection can miss a package-level state change. A primitive may fit a local mesh
region and still shift the aggregate body state when assembled with the rest of the package. This
section gives the derivation used to explain that mechanism.

\paragraph{Aggregate center of mass.}
For primitive components with masses $m_i$ and centers $c_i$ expressed in a common body frame, the
compound mass and center of mass are
\[
  M = \sum_i m_i,
  \qquad
  c = \frac{1}{M}\sum_i m_i c_i.
\]
The formula is simple, but its diagnostic implication is important. A large flat primitive can move
the aggregate center even if its isolated local fit looks plausible. The body state seen by Newton
is the compound package, not the individual primitive viewed alone.

\paragraph{Aggregate inertia.}
Let $J_i$ be the inertia of primitive $i$ about its own center in the shared body orientation. The
parallel-axis theorem moves each inertia to the aggregate center:
\[
  J = \sum_i \left(J_i + m_i(\|d_i\|^2 I - d_i d_i^\top)\right),
  \qquad d_i = c_i - c.
\]
The term $m_i(\|d_i\|^2 I - d_i d_i^\top)$ is the body-state sensitivity term. It grows with mass
and squared distance from the aggregate center. The diagnostic checker does not need to prove that
this term is the only possible cause of every failure. It only needs to catch packages whose
recorded simulated state violates the configured predicate.

\paragraph{Why isolated success is insufficient.}
Suppose primitive $p_j$ passes an isolated drop/settle probe:
\[
  A_{\mathrm{drop}}(\{p_j\})=1.
\]
It does not follow that
\[
  A_{\mathrm{drop}}(P_g)=1,
\]
because the full package $P_g$ changes $M$, $c$, $J$, contact layout, and support geometry. The
acceptance layer therefore evaluates the whole package under the task, not only each primitive's
local residual.

\paragraph{What this does not show.}
The figure does not claim that COM/inertia sensitivity explains every primitive-package failure,
and it does not claim a validated COM or inertia repair method.

\paragraph{Audit recipe.}
When a reviewer sees a package-level body-state failure, the useful audit questions are:
(i)~was the package actually generated and mapped, (ii)~did the isolated component pass a simpler
probe, (iii)~did the full package change terminal speed, support height, or floor breach, and
(iv)~does the record preserve the fallback or failure label? These questions are stronger than a
visual overlay alone because they connect the geometry to the executable scene.
